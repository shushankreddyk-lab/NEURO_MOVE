Abstract

The classification of Motor Imagery Electroencephalogram (MI-EEG) signals represents a formidable challenge in Brain-Computer Interface (BCI) design due to the inherent nonstationarity, low signal-to-noise ratio, and severe inter-subject variability characterizing cortical electrical activity. While deep learning architectures, particularly Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks, have established strong classification baselines, their computational latency and vulnerability to overfitting remain prohibitive for real-world assistive technologies. This project reproduces and significantly extends a recent benchmark study [1] that demonstrated the superiority of the Minimally Random Convolutional Kernel Transform (MiniRocket) over a 13-layer CNN-LSTM hybrid. Although MiniRocket achieved 98.63% accuracy on the PhysioNet dataset, the base literature identified a critical gap: the lack of non-linear spatial coalition modeling across the motor cortex. To address this, we introduce a novel Choquet-Integral electrode fusion mechanism applied symmetrically across motor-cortex electrode pairs (C3/C4, C1/C2, FC3/FC4, CP3/CP4) to capture synergistic channel interactions. Furthermore, we extend the traditional 4-class MI paradigm by bifurcating the ambiguous "Both Feet" class into independent "Left Foot" and "Right Foot" classifications, demanding a much higher degree of laterality resolution. By imposing rigorous subject-independent train/test protocols, this extended framework aims to not only match the 98% accuracy ceiling of the base model but to vastly improve cross-subject generalizability and real-time robotic prosthetic actuation for ALS and stroke rehabilitation.
Keywords: Motor Imagery, EEG, BCI, MiniRocket, Choquet Integral, CNN-LSTM.

1. Introduction

A Brain-Computer Interface (BCI) establishes a direct, non-muscular communication pathway between the human brain and external computing systems, fundamentally bypassing the peripheral nervous system [1]. This technology serves as a critical assistive lifeline for patients suffering from severe motor disabilities, including Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis (ALS), spinal cord injuries, and severe stroke [10]. Among non-invasive BCI modalities, Electroencephalography (EEG) remains the most widely deployed due to its high temporal resolution, portability, and relatively low cost compared to functional Magnetic Resonance Imaging (fMRI) or Magnetoencephalography (MEG) [11]. Within EEG-based BCIs, Motor Imagery (MI) paradigms are particularly valuable. Motor imagery involves the mental execution of a movement without any actual muscle contraction, which triggers localized Event-Related Desynchronization (ERD) and Event-Related Synchronization (ERS) in the sensorimotor cortex, primarily within the mu (8–14 Hz) and beta (14–30 Hz) frequency bands [12].

Despite decades of research, the robust classification of MI-EEG signals remains a highly constrained pattern recognition problem. EEG signals are notoriously nonstationary; their statistical properties evolve unpredictably over time even within the same subject [1]. Furthermore, the signals are characterized by an extremely low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR), often heavily contaminated by biological artifacts such as electrooculogram (EOG) blink interference and electromyogram (EMG) muscle twitches. To navigate this complexity, researchers have increasingly turned to deep learning architectures. Specifically, hybrid models combining Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for spatial feature extraction [18] and Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks [21] for temporal sequence modeling have achieved state-of-the-art results [23]. However, the computational overhead required to optimize the millions of parameters within a CNN-LSTM network renders rapid bedside calibration practically impossible [1].

To circumvent the latency bottleneck of deep neural networks, recent foundational work [1] proposed the application of the Minimally Random Convolutional Kernel Transform (MiniRocket) [2] to MI-EEG classification. MiniRocket Abandons iterative backpropagation in favor of computing the Proportion of Positive Values (PPV) across thousands of deterministic convolutional kernels, yielding a highly dense linear feature space that can be instantly mapped via a Ridge classifier. While the base paper [1] achieved an impressive 98.63% on the PhysioNet dataset [4] and 92.57% on BCI Competition IV 2a, it treated all spatial electrodes uniformly. As explicitly noted in their conclusion, the framework lacked a mechanism to fuse information across synergistic electrode coalitions, limiting its theoretical ceiling.

In this project, we address the exact limitations identified by the base literature [1]. We propose a hybrid architecture that integrates the raw speed of MiniRocket [2] with the temporal depth of CNN-LSTM networks [24], governed by a novel Choquet-Integral spatial fusion layer [15]. Unlike standard linear combinations (e.g., average pooling or fully connected layers), the Choquet Integral operates over fuzzy measures, allowing the model to explicitly capture both redundant and synergistic relationships between specific contralateral electrode pairs (such as C3 on the left hemisphere and C4 on the right hemisphere) [3]. This non-additive fusion mathematically mirrors the biological reality of cross-hemispheric motor planning.

Furthermore, standard MI-BCI paradigms, including those utilized in the base paper [1], typically group foot movements into a single "Both Feet" class due to the spatial proximity of the lower limb representations in the central longitudinal fissure of the brain. We explicitly decouple this into "Left Foot" and "Right Foot" classes, challenging the Choquet fusion mechanism to resolve highly fine-grained limb laterality. This project, therefore, is not merely a reproduction, but a comprehensive structural and functional extension of the current benchmark.

2. Literature Review

The evolution of Motor Imagery BCI classification has been characterized by a continuous transition from handcrafted spatial filtering techniques to automated deep learning representation extraction, and most recently, back to highly efficient deterministic mathematical transforms.

Paper [1]: Hwaidi and Ghanem (2026), "Motor imagery EEG signal classification using minimally random convolutional kernel transform and hybrid deep learning." This serves as the foundational base paper for our project. The authors conducted an exhaustive comparison between the MiniRocket algorithm [2] and a densely parameterized 13-layer CNN-LSTM hybrid. Their preprocessing pipeline utilized Independent Component Analysis (ICA) to isolate the mu (8-14 Hz) and beta (14-30 Hz) frequency bands. Evaluated on the PhysioNet dataset [4] (109 subjects, 64 channels), their CNN-LSTM achieved 98.06% accuracy, while MiniRocket achieved a superior 98.63%. On the BCI Competition IV 2a dataset, MiniRocket achieved 92.57%. Critically, MiniRocket achieved these results without requiring GPU acceleration, completing training in seconds rather than hours. The authors concluded by explicitly identifying the need for advanced, non-linear electrode coalition fusion as a future research vector, which directly motivates the integration of Choquet integrals in Section 3 of our work.

Paper [2]: Wu, S.-L. et al., "Fuzzy Integral With Particle Swarm Optimization for CNN-Based Motor-Imagery EEG Classification." This research addressed the limitations of traditional linear decision fusion in ensemble neural networks. By deploying Choquet and Sugeno fuzzy integrals, the authors fused the outputs of multiple independent CNN classifiers trained on different spatial domains of the EEG. The fuzzy measures were optimized using Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO). The study proved that non-additive fusion could mathematically model the interaction between different cortical regions better than standard soft-voting mechanisms. This insight is foundational to our approach, as we adapt this fuzzy integral logic not for ensemble output fusion, but for direct fusion of symmetric motor-cortex electrode pairs (e.g., C3/C4) prior to final classification.

Paper [3]: Xu et al., "Study on EEG Recognition Framework by Common Spatial Pattern and Fuzzy Fusion." In this PubMed-indexed study, the authors tackled the inherent variance of standard Common Spatial Pattern (CSP) filters. They constructed a multi-resolution CSP pipeline where the outputs of Linear Discriminant Analysis (LDA) classifiers were aggregated using a Choquet integral fusion module. The authors demonstrated statistically significant accuracy gains over standard CSP by exploiting the synergistic information between different frequency sub-bands. This paper reinforces the mathematical viability of fuzzy measure theory in physiological signal processing, motivating our decision to replace standard dense linear layers with Choquet integration [3] to handle the highly correlated, yet distinct, signals emanating from the left and right motor cortices during limb laterality tasks.

Paper [4]: Goldberger et al. and Schalk et al., "PhysioBank, PhysioToolkit, and PhysioNet: Components of a new research resource for complex physiologic signals." This paper documents the creation and structural specifications of the PhysioNet EEG Motor Movement/Imagery Dataset, which serves as the primary evaluation corpus for both the base paper [1] and our extended project. The dataset was captured using the BCI2000 system, encompassing 109 subjects recorded across 64 high-density electrodes at 160Hz. Understanding the exact paradigm timings detailed in this source material is absolutely critical for the precise temporal epoching applied during our preprocessing phase, ensuring that the model captures the exact moment of Event-Related Desynchronization (ERD) without bleeding into the inter-trial rest periods.

Paper [5]: Dempster, Schmidt, and Webb, "MINIROCKET: A very fast (almost) deterministic transform for time series classification." This is the original publication introducing the MiniRocket architecture. The authors demonstrated that by stripping the randomness out of the original ROCKET algorithm and utilizing a fixed set of 84 mathematically optimized convolutional kernels, they could achieve state-of-the-art accuracy on the UCR Time Series Classification Archive. Because it relies on the Proportion of Positive Values (PPV) rather than gradient descent backpropagation, it trains orders of magnitude faster than ResNet or InceptionTime. We rely heavily on the mathematical proofs in this paper to justify our implementation of the MiniRocket pipeline [1] as the primary feature extractor for our real-time BCI framework.

Beyond these primary anchors, our work is supported by a broad spectrum of fundamental machine learning and neurophysiology literature. The deployment of the CNN-LSTM hybrid [1] necessitates foundational citations for Convolutional Neural Networks [18] and the original formulation of the Long Short-Term Memory architecture by Hochreiter and Schmidhuber [21]. The mechanics of backpropagation, ReLU activation, and Softmax normalization [22] are critical components of the deep learning baseline. Furthermore, our spatial preprocessing pipeline relies on the foundational mathematics of Fast Independent Component Analysis (ICA) [25] for artifact rejection, separating biologically distinct source generators (such as eye blinks) from the underlying cortical motor planning [26]. By synthesizing the rapid feature extraction of MiniRocket [5], the temporal depth of LSTMs [21], and the non-additive synergy modeling of the Choquet Integral [2], our proposed methodology represents a comprehensively grounded advancement in BCI literature.

3. Proposed Methodology

3.1 Overview

The proposed framework establishes a highly robust pipeline for Motor Imagery EEG classification, directly extending the work of Hwaidi and Ghanem [1]. As depicted in the workflow diagram (Figure 1), the architecture ingests raw, 64-channel continuous EEG streams. The signal undergoes rigorous spatial and temporal preprocessing to isolate the neurological frequencies of interest. Following this, the data stream splits into a dual-pathway feature extraction module: the highly deterministic MiniRocket transform [5] and a 13-layer CNN-LSTM hybrid [24]. The extracted spatial-temporal feature matrices are then fed into our primary novel contribution: a Choquet-Integral fusion layer [2] that explicitly models the nonlinear synergistic interactions across contralateral electrode pairs. Finally, a linear classifier outputs the fine-grained limb laterality prediction.

3.2 Preprocessing

Raw EEG signals are contaminated by high-amplitude biological artifacts (EOG, EMG) and environmental interference (powerline noise). Following the exact protocols established in the base paper [1], the raw signals are first passed through a 50/60 Hz notch filter to eliminate alternating current interference. A Common Average Reference (CAR) spatial filter is applied to remove global background noise. Subsequently, a 4th-order zero-phase Butterworth bandpass filter is applied between 4 Hz and 40 Hz, which safely encompasses the critical mu (8–14 Hz) and beta (14–30 Hz) sensorimotor rhythms responsible for motor planning [12].

To further isolate the cortical sources, Independent Component Analysis (ICA) [25] is deployed to blindly separate the mixed EEG signals into statistically independent components. Components exhibiting topographical maps localized to the frontal poles (indicative of eye blinks) are mathematically zeroed out before the signal is reconstructed. Finally, temporal epochs are extracted from -0.5 seconds to +4.0 seconds relative to the visual cue onset, capturing both the baseline resting state and the active motor imagery execution phase [4].

3.3 Approach 1 — MiniRocket

The first pathway utilizes the Minimally Random Convolutional Kernel Transform (MiniRocket) [5]. Unlike deep neural networks, MiniRocket does not learn features via iterative backpropagation. Instead, it convolves the time-series EEG epoch with a fixed, deterministic set of 10,000 carefully constructed convolutional kernels of length 9. These kernels exhaustively scan the signal for specific waveform morphologies. Once convolved, the most critical step is the pooling operation. MiniRocket calculates the Proportion of Positive Values (PPV) for each feature map, compressing the temporal sequence into a highly dense scalar feature vector [1]. The mathematical formulation for the PPV calculation is expressed natively below:

$$ \text{PPV} = \frac{1}{m} \sum_{i=1}^{m} [x_i \cdot c_i + b > 0] $$

Where \( m \) represents the length of the convolved sequence, \( x_i \) is the input segment, \( c_i \) is the fixed kernel weight, \( b \) is the deterministic bias, and the bracket notation denotes the Iverson bracket (evaluating to 1 if the condition is true, 0 otherwise). This generated 10,000-dimensional feature space is then fed into a highly optimized Ridge Regression classifier with L2 regularization.

3.4 Approach 2 — CNN-LSTM

To capture deep hierarchical spatial patterns and long-range temporal dependencies, a 13-layer Convolutional Neural Network coupled with a Long Short-Term Memory network (CNN-LSTM) is deployed as the second parallel extraction pathway [1]. The initial layers consist of 1D Convolutions utilizing 16 and 32 filters, followed by Batch Normalization and MaxPooling to extract localized frequency-domain motifs. To prevent overfitting, severe dropout rates of 0.5 and 0.25 are enforced across the dense layers. The network is optimized using the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of \(1 \times 10^{-5}\) and L2 weight decay of 0.01.

The core temporal modeling is handled by twin LSTM layers, each containing 100 hidden units [21]. The LSTM explicitly solves the vanishing gradient problem inherent in standard RNNs by maintaining an internal cell state modulated by three distinct non-linear gates. The native mathematical equations governing the forward pass of the LSTM are explicitly defined as follows:

$$ f_t = \sigma(W_f \cdot [h_{t-1}, x_t] + b_f) $$
$$ i_t = \sigma(W_i \cdot [h_{t-1}, x_t] + b_i) $$
$$ C_t = f_t * C_{t-1} + i_t * \tanh(W_c \cdot [h_{t-1}, x_t] + b_c) $$
$$ o_t = \sigma(W_o \cdot [h_{t-1}, x_t] + b_o) $$
$$ h_t = o_t * \tanh(C_t) $$

Where \( f_t \) is the forget gate, \( i_t \) is the input gate, \( C_t \) is the cell state, \( o_t \) is the output gate, and \( h_t \) is the hidden state passed to the subsequent time step. The final hidden state is flattened and passed into dense layers (100, 50, and 4 neurons) for baseline probabilistic prediction via a Softmax activation.

3.5 OUR NOVELTY — Choquet-Integral Electrode Fusion

While the base models process the entire 64-channel array uniformly, neurophysiological motor planning dictates that specific contralateral electrode pairs (e.g., C3 over the left motor cortex and C4 over the right) exhibit highly correlated, synergistic behaviors during limb laterality tasks. Standard neural network dense layers model these interactions via simple linear addition. This project introduces a non-additive aggregation layer utilizing the Choquet Integral [2][3], which can mathematically model redundancies (where \( 1+1 < 2 \)) and synergies (where \( 1+1 > 2 \)) across defined electrode coalitions.

The Choquet Integral evaluates a function with respect to a fuzzy measure \( \mu \). For a given sorted input vector from our symmetric electrode pairs, the Choquet Integral equation is defined natively as:

$$ C_\mu(f) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} [f(x_{(i)}) - f(x_{(i-1)})] \cdot \mu(A_{(i)}) $$

Where \( f \) represents the extracted feature values sorted in ascending order such that \( f(x_{(1)}) \leq f(x_{(2)}) \dots \leq f(x_{(n)}) \), with \( f(x_{(0)}) = 0 \). The term \( \mu(A_{(i)}) \) represents the fuzzy measure applied to the coalition of electrodes \( A_{(i)} = \{x_{(i)}, \dots, x_{(n)}\} \). This fusion explicitly forces the model to weigh the combined interactive states of C3/C4 and FC3/FC4 heavily when making unilateral limb predictions.

3.6 OUR NOVELTY — Foot Laterality Extension

The base paper [1], alongside the vast majority of PhysioNet evaluation protocols, simplifies the classification task by merging left foot and right foot motor imagery into a single "Both Feet" generic class. This is primarily because foot representations in the primary motor cortex are located deeply within the central longitudinal fissure, making lateralized surface EEG detection exceedingly difficult. To push the boundaries of BCI precision, this project explicitly extends the classification paradigm. We split the "Both Feet" data blocks, forcing the classification engine to distinguish between Left Foot and Right Foot independently. This fine-grained laterality resolution relies heavily on the contralateral ERD signatures amplified by the Choquet-Integral fusion layer [3].

4. Dataset Description

The primary corpus utilized to validate the proposed Choquet-fused architecture is the PhysioNet EEG Motor Movement/Imagery Dataset [4]. Recorded utilizing the highly standardized BCI2000 system, the dataset captures the cortical activity of 109 healthy subjects. The electrical activity is recorded across 64 high-density EEG channels aligned with the international 10-10 system, sampled continuously at 160 Hz. The raw dataset and all corresponding EDF files are publicly accessible via the PhysioNet repository at: https://physionet.org/content/eegmmidb/1.0.0/.

The experimental protocol dictates that each subject completes 14 distinct runs. The initial runs (R01, R02) establish resting baseline metrics with eyes open and eyes closed, respectively. The subsequent runs alternate between actual execution of movements and the pure mental motor imagery of those exact movements. For this classification study, we strictly isolate the motor imagery runs. Runs R04, R08, and R12 contain the Left Fist and Right Fist motor imagery paradigms. Runs R06, R10, and R14 contain the "Both Fists" and "Both Feet" imagery tasks. In our extended novelty protocol, the "Both Feet" markers are split to evaluate Left Foot vs Right Foot laterality. The exact mapping of the BCI2000 event markers (T0, T1, T2) is defined comprehensively in Table 2.

This dataset contains 22-channel EEG recordings from 9 subjects performing four classes of motor imagery (left hand, right hand, feet, and tongue), providing a critical baseline to confirm that the Choquet-Integral fusion mechanisms are not overfitted exclusively to the 64-channel PhysioNet topography.

6. Conclusion & Future Work

This project successfully outlines a comprehensive reproduction and strategic extension of the state-of-the-art MI-EEG classification benchmark established by Hwaidi and Ghanem [1]. While the base paper irrefutably demonstrated the raw computational and accuracy superiority of the deterministic MiniRocket transform over heavily parameterized CNN-LSTM deep learning hybrids, it ultimately left the spatial integration of contralateral motor cortex signals unresolved. By introducing a Choquet-Integral electrode fusion module, we establish a mathematically rigorous methodology for aggregating synergistic EEG data across the C3/C4 and FC3/FC4 hemispheres. Furthermore, by dismantling the generalized "Both Feet" class into fine-grained Left Foot and Right Foot laterality classifications, we significantly escalate the precision demanded of the BCI framework, pushing it closer to the resolution required for dexterous neuro-prosthetic control.

Future work will pivot on three distinct axes. First, the completed algorithmic pipeline will undergo exhaustive statistical validation on the full BCI Competition IV 2a dataset to confirm that the Choquet fuzzy measures generalize across highly varied electrode densities (22 vs 64 channels). Second, rigorous real-time latency testing will be conducted on micro-controller hardware to ensure the Choquet integration overhead does not exceed the 100-millisecond perceptual synchrony threshold required for closed-loop robotic actuation. Finally, future iterations will explore dynamic, adaptive coalition formulations for the Choquet measure, utilizing Particle Swarm Optimization to dynamically redefine the synergistic electrode pairings based on the subject's localized anatomical fatigue during extended BCI sessions.
