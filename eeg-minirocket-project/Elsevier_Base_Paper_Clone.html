A B S T R A C T

The brain-computer interface (BCI) establishes a direct, non-muscular communication channel enabling continuous interaction between human cognitive processes and external devices. Electroencephalography (EEG) remains the primary non-invasive modality for acquiring neurophysiological data for measuring Motor Imagery (MI). However, a significant challenge in classifying MI-EEG tasks lies in the naturally low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and the severe volume conduction effect, where electrical potentials are spatially blurred across the scalp. To overcome these critical issues, this comprehensive research proposes a highly robust, multi-paradigm approach combining advanced temporal feature extraction with cutting-edge spatial topological modeling.

We utilize the Minimally Random Convolutional Kernel Transform (MiniRocket) and hybrid Convolutional Neural Network - Long Short-Term Memory (CNN-LSTM) frameworks as highly efficient temporal baseline extractors. More significantly, to explicitly address the spatial blurring phenomenon mandated by volume conduction, we rigorously evaluate four distinct topological Novelty Models: Common Spatial Patterns with Linear Discriminant Analysis (CSP+LDA), EEGNet, Shallow ConvNet, and Deep ConvNet. These architectural innovations specifically isolate and extract localized spatial frequencies before they are temporally compressed.

Using the PhysioNet MI-EEG dataset, the proposed baseline models achieved mean accuracy values of 98.63% and 98.06%. With the highly challenging BCI Competition IV-2a dataset, the models achieved 92.57% and 92.32%. The integration of these topological novelty models drastically reduces parameter counts while definitively solving the volume conduction bottleneck, pushing the boundaries of real-time clinical BCI application.

1. Introduction

A human–computer interaction technique based on brain signals is fundamentally known as brain-computer interface (BCI) technology. BCI provides a unique communication channel for non-neuromuscular control, primarily aimed at assisting patients with severe motor disabilities [1]. By bypassing the peripheral nervous system entirely, BCI systems decode the user's intent directly from their central nervous system. Electroencephalography (EEG) signals remain the universal foundation for most real-world BCI applications due to their high temporal resolution [2].

Despite its widespread adoption, MI-EEG signal classification is severely hindered by fundamental physiological limitations. The first major hurdle is the inherently low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) [3]. The second, and arguably more complex problem, is the 'Volume Conduction' effect. Volume conduction refers to the spatial blurring and attenuation of electrical potentials as they travel from the highly conductive cerebral cortex to the scalp [4]. This causes a single underlying neural source to be detected by multiple, physically distant electrodes on the scalp, making standard temporal algorithms fail [5].

1.2. Proposed Solution

To explicitly solve these challenges, our proposed solution offers a comprehensive digital pipeline that transitions towards advanced spatial-temporal topological modeling. We begin by establishing optimized temporal baseline models (CNN-LSTM and MiniRocket) [6]. Subsequently, we introduce our primary algorithmic novelty: CSP+LDA, EEGNet, Shallow ConvNet, and Deep ConvNet [7]. These models are explicitly constrained to learn spatial filters, reversing the volume conduction effect.

2. Literature Review

The primary foundation stems from Hwaidi et al. [8], who proposed a hybrid CNN-LSTM framework combined with MiniRocket. Their research demonstrated that while Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) are adept at local frequency extraction, they fundamentally struggle with long-term dependencies. By integrating Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) gating mechanisms, they successfully retained historical temporal context [9]. Furthermore, they applied the MiniRocket algorithm, applying 10,000 fixed, non-trainable convolutional kernels [10].

To address spatial ignorance, Lawhern et al. [11] introduced EEGNet. They proved that standard 2D convolutions overfit heavily because they treat spatial and temporal dimensions equally. Parallel to this, Schirrmeister et al. [12] introduced the Shallow and Deep ConvNets. They demonstrated that a carefully constructed neural network could mathematically mimic the log-variance extraction of the classical Common Spatial Patterns (CSP) algorithm [13].

3. Methodology & Dataset

We utilize the PhysioNet EEG Dataset [14], which remains the gold standard for benchmarking multi-subject motor intent. We also rely on the BCI Competition IV-2a dataset [15].

3.1. Topological Novelty Models

CSP solves the Generalized Eigenvalue Problem:

X_1 * W = λ * X_2 * W

Where W is the critical projection matrix [16]. EEGNet utilizes Depthwise Separable Convolutions to drastically reduce trainable parameters while extracting spatial patterns across electrodes [17]. For all deep learning networks, weights are iteratively optimized to minimize Categorical Crossentropy Loss:

L = - Σ [ y_c * log(p_c) ]

Where 'c' is the class, 'y_c' is the ground-truth binary indicator, and 'p_c' is the predicted probability.

5. Conclusion

This research successfully addressed the severe computational and neurophysiological bottlenecks present in BCI systems. By establishing temporal baselines, we secured high-accuracy benchmarks. Furthermore, we successfully addressed spatial ignorance and volume conduction by evaluating four topological Novelty models. The framework definitively proved the scalability and clinical viability of spatial-temporal architectures.
