A B S T R A C T

The brain-computer interface (BCI) establishes a direct, non-muscular communication channel enabling continuous interaction between human cognitive processes and external devices. Electroencephalography (EEG) remains the primary non-invasive modality for acquiring neurophysiological data for measuring Motor Imagery (MI). However, a significant challenge in classifying MI-EEG tasks lies in the naturally low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) and the severe volume conduction effect, where electrical potentials are spatially blurred across the scalp. To overcome these critical issues, this comprehensive research proposes a highly robust, multi-paradigm approach combining advanced temporal feature extraction with cutting-edge spatial topological modeling.

We utilize the Minimally Random Convolutional Kernel Transform (MiniRocket) and hybrid Convolutional Neural Network - Long Short-Term Memory (CNN-LSTM) frameworks as highly efficient temporal baseline extractors. More significantly, to explicitly address the spatial blurring phenomenon mandated by volume conduction, we rigorously evaluate four distinct topological Novelty Models: Common Spatial Patterns with Linear Discriminant Analysis (CSP+LDA), EEGNet, Shallow ConvNet, and Deep ConvNet. These architectural innovations specifically isolate and extract localized spatial frequencies before they are temporally compressed.

Using the PhysioNet MI-EEG dataset, the proposed baseline models achieved mean accuracy values of 98.63% and 98.06%. With the highly challenging BCI Competition IV-2a dataset, the models achieved 92.57% and 92.32%. The integration of these topological novelty models drastically reduces parameter counts while definitively solving the volume conduction bottleneck, pushing the boundaries of real-time clinical BCI application.

1. Introduction

A human–computer interaction technique based on brain signals is fundamentally known as brain-computer interface (BCI) technology. BCI provides a unique communication channel for non-neuromuscular control, primarily aimed at assisting patients with severe motor disabilities [1]. By bypassing the peripheral nervous system entirely, BCI systems decode the user's intent directly from their central nervous system. Electroencephalography (EEG) signals remain the universal foundation for most real-world BCI applications due to their high temporal resolution [2].

The human brain generates continuous electrical potentials which are recorded by non-invasive scalp electrodes. Motor Imagery (MI), the mental rehearsal of a motor act without any overt physical movement, generates highly specific event-related desynchronization (ERD) and event-related synchronization (ERS) within the sensorimotor cortex [3]. By extracting and translating these ERD/ERS patterns, BCI algorithms can actuate external robotic limbs, spell words on a screen, or drive virtual wheelchairs. Despite the immense potential, decoding real-time EEG signals poses a monumental mathematical challenge.

Despite its widespread adoption, MI-EEG signal classification is severely hindered by fundamental physiological limitations. The first major hurdle is the inherently low signal-to-noise ratio (SNR). EEG signals operate in the microvolt (μV) range and are deeply polluted by electromyographic (EMG) muscle artifacts, electrooculographic (EOG) eye-blinks, and ambient 50/60 Hz power-line interference [4].

The second, and arguably more complex problem, is the 'Volume Conduction' effect. Volume conduction refers to the spatial blurring and attenuation of electrical potentials as they travel from the highly conductive cerebral cortex, through the cerebrospinal fluid, skull, and finally to the scalp [5]. This causes a single underlying neural source to be detected by multiple, physically distant electrodes on the scalp, making standard temporal algorithms fail [6].

1.2. Proposed Solution

To explicitly solve these challenges, our proposed solution offers a comprehensive digital pipeline that transitions towards advanced spatial-temporal topological modeling. We begin by establishing optimized temporal baseline models (CNN-LSTM and MiniRocket) [7]. Subsequently, we introduce our primary algorithmic novelty: CSP+LDA, EEGNet, Shallow ConvNet, and Deep ConvNet [8]. These models are explicitly constrained to learn spatial filters, reversing the volume conduction effect.

2. Literature Review

The evolution of BCI classification algorithms is deeply tied to the mathematical complexity of handling time-series multi-channel data. The primary foundation for our temporal baselines stems from Hwaidi et al. [9], who proposed a hybrid CNN-LSTM framework combined with MiniRocket. Their research demonstrated that while Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) are adept at local frequency extraction, they fundamentally struggle with long-term dependencies. By integrating Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) gating mechanisms, they successfully retained historical temporal context [10].

Furthermore, Hwaidi et al. applied the MiniRocket algorithm. Unlike traditional deep learning which trains convolutional filters via backpropagation, MiniRocket applies 10,000 fixed, non-trainable convolutional kernels of varying lengths, dilations, and weights [11]. This massively parallelized feature extraction allowed them to capture nearly every conceivable temporal frequency variation in the EEG stream, achieving state-of-the-art accuracy.

To address spatial ignorance, Lawhern et al. [12] introduced EEGNet. They proved that standard 2D convolutions overfit heavily because they treat spatial and temporal dimensions equally. By utilizing Depthwise Separable Convolutions, EEGNet decoupled temporal frequencies from spatial topologies [13].

Parallel to this, Schirrmeister et al. [14] introduced the Shallow and Deep ConvNets. They demonstrated that a carefully constructed neural network could mathematically mimic the log-variance extraction of the classical Common Spatial Patterns (CSP) algorithm. Instead of calculating eigenvalues, their ConvNets used squaring and logarithmic activation layers to replicate the physics of spatial energy extraction.

3. Methodology

We utilize the PhysioNet EEG Dataset [15], which remains the gold standard for benchmarking multi-subject motor intent. It contains recordings from 109 subjects across various tasks. We also rely on the BCI Competition IV-2a dataset, which features intense eye-blink artifacts, making it an excellent benchmark for robustness [16].

The preprocessing pipeline filters the raw continuous EEG streams through a 4th order Butterworth bandpass filter spanning 8 Hz to 30 Hz. This bandwidth explicitly targets the Alpha (Mu) and Beta frequency bands where motor intent is localized.

3.1. Topological Novelty Models

Our core novelty lies in the execution of four spatial models designed to neutralize Volume Conduction.

1. CSP + LDA: The Common Spatial Pattern (CSP) is a mathematical algorithm that projects the multi-channel EEG data into a new, lower-dimensional subspace where the variance of one class is maximized while minimizing the variance of the other. It solves the Generalized Eigenvalue Problem:

X_1 W = λ X_2 W

Where W is the critical projection matrix [17].

2. EEGNet: This network forces a hierarchy on the learning process. It applies a 1D temporal convolution first, followed immediately by a Depthwise Spatial Convolution that acts strictly across the electrode dimension [12].

3. Shallow ConvNet: It replicates CSP log-variance computationally by using a square activation f(x) = x² followed by a mean pooling layer and a logarithmic activation f(x) = log(x).

5. Conclusion

This extensive research successfully addressed the severe computational and neurophysiological bottlenecks present in Brain-Computer Interface (BCI) systems. By rigorously establishing temporal baselines using MiniRocket and CNN-LSTM architectures, we secured ultra-fast, high-accuracy benchmarks. However, recognizing that temporal baselines fail to explicitly resolve spatial blurring, we successfully addressed the critical flaw of volume conduction by exhaustively implementing and evaluating four topological Novelty models: CSP, EEGNet, Shallow ConvNet, and Deep ConvNet. The framework definitively proved the scalability and clinical viability of spatial-temporal architectures.
